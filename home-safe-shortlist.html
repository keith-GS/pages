Buy the AMSEC CFX352020 AmVaultx6 (TL-30x6). It is the single best ratio of insurance coverage to dollar in this market. The Insurance Math (this drives the whole decision)

The UL rating on your safe directly caps how much value insurers will write coverage on at home. Underbuy and you are self-insuring the difference. Most people learn this after a loss.

Installation, Delivery, Anchoring

For a 1,500-3,500 lb safe going into a basement, you are not hiring two guys with a hand truck from a moving company. You want a professional safe-mover (most safe dealers have an in-house crew or partner with one). Get a written quote with a stair fee disclosed up front.

Standard Delivery

$300 - $600

Ground-floor garage drop-off. Most dealers offer "free shipping" but it means curbside.

White-Glove Placement

$200 - $500

Inside placement, level, position in final spot. Add to standard delivery.

Basement Stair Fee

$300 - $1,200

Charged per flight, per number of stairs, by weight. Heavier = more. NYC end of range higher.

Concrete Anchoring

$150 - $400

4-5 epoxy anchors into the slab. Gold standard for $1M contents. Need 4000+ PSI concrete.

Floor Reinforcement (if needed)

$0 - $800

Most basement slabs handle 2,000-3,500 lbs fine. Older floors may need spreader plates.

Monitored Alarm Tie-In

$200 - $600 + monthly

Required by most insurers to unlock max coverage. Vibration sensor on safe + central station monitoring.

My Take

If this were my decision, I'd buy the AMSEC CFX352020 today and call my insurance broker before I bought it. Here is why:

The rating matters more than the brand. A $25K Brown Safe Gem in TL-15 is a worse insurance asset than an $11K AMSEC CFX352020 in TL-30x6. Pretty interior, lower insurable value. The luxury jewelry-safe market trades on craftsmanship and aesthetics; insurance carriers trade on UL ratings. Buy to the rating.

"Bank-level" is a real category, and you do not need it. True bank-vault safes are TRTL-30x6 (tool AND torch resistant) and start at $25K-$50K. For $1M of jewelry kept in a private home with a monitored alarm and a properly anchored TL-30x6, you are well past the carrier's risk threshold. Spending more on burglary rating from here is diminishing returns.

Three things that quietly matter more than which safe you pick:

  1. Monitored central-station alarm with vibration/seismic sensor on the safe itself. Most carriers double or triple your coverage cap when this is in place. Cost: $25-60/month.
  2. A current jewelry appraisal (within 3 years) for every piece on a scheduled rider. Without scheduled riders, your homeowner's policy probably caps jewelry at $1,500-5,000 total. Schedule everything.
  3. Don't put it in the obvious place. A 2,000 lb safe in the middle of a basement is great. A 2,000 lb safe behind a bookcase, in a utility closet, or in a finished room that does not look like the "safe room" is much better. Conceal first, anchor second.

One more thing - get the appraisal done first. If the collection is actually $1M, schedule it. If it is $400K with sentimental value that feels like $1M, that changes the math significantly (the CF6528 or even Hayman MV30 might be enough). Knowing the real number lets you buy the right safe instead of over-buying out of caution or under-buying out of cost shock.

Quick Action List

  1. Call your insurance broker this week. Ask: "For $X of scheduled jewelry kept at home, what UL safe rating do you require for full coverage?" Get the answer in writing.
  2. Get a current appraisal if you don't have one from the last 3 years. Most jewelers do appraisals for $50-150 per piece, or charge ~1% of value for the full collection.
  3. Request 3 quotes for the CFX352020 from regional safe dealers (Dean Safe, Maximum Security, AMSEC dealer locator). Prices vary $2K-4K on the same safe.
  4. Confirm basement access with the dealer's install crew BEFORE ordering. Send them photos of the stairs and basement door dimensions. Get the stair fee in writing.
  5. Confirm slab can hold ~2,000 lbs. Most modern basements are fine. Older NYC brownstone basements with brick floors are not - you'd need a steel spreader plate.
  6. Schedule alarm install for the same day or week. Tell the alarm company you want vibration/seismic sensing on the safe and central station monitoring.
