Feature Spotlight · New this week.

Exit scenario (Beta): if the company sold, who gets what

Ownership percentage times price is not what you get at exit. The answer runs through the preference stack, the shadow series that converted at a discount, the option pool, and whichever classes are better off converting to common at that price. The new Scenarios tab does that arithmetic for any company in your portfolio, automatically, from the charter and cap table we already extracted: set an exit value, read proceeds to you, gain or loss, MOIC and IRR, and drag the marker across exit values to see where the story changes. This is the simple view; more scenarios are coming. If there is one you want first, reply and tell us.

Keith's Note

There is a question every GP gets asked and almost nobody can answer on the spot: if this company sold tomorrow, what would we actually get? Most of us answer with ownership times price, and most of us are wrong, because the real answer runs through a preference stack that the charter wrote and the cap table filled in. As an example, one company we tested this week holds six sub-series of the same round, each converted at a different discount; at last-round post-money every one of them converts to common, and what comes back to the fund is well above what cost basis suggests. We started with the exit scenario because it is the version of the waterfall a GP needs most often and trusts least, and we labelled it Beta because a waterfall earns trust one company at a time. Waterfalls have a way of humbling anyone who thinks they can run one in their head; I include myself.
