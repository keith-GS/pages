Feature Spotlight · New this week.

SOC 2 Type 2: your documents, cap tables and marks behind independently tested controls

GoodStream completed a SOC 2 Type 2 examination covering the Security Trust Services Criteria for the period March 25 to June 25, 2026. An independent CPA firm tested the design and operating effectiveness of our controls and issued an unqualified opinion; the report is available under NDA. Every document you hand us is somebody’s confidential IP, so this is the standard we hold ourselves to - and LLMs will never train on your data. If a vendor due diligence questionnaire lands on your desk, the answer is ready: Security page · Subprocessors · Request the report (or write to security@goodstream.com).

Keith's Note

This spring we had a choice between a SOC 2 Type 1, which checks that the controls exist on a single day, and a Type 2, which tests whether they actually operated over a period. We went straight to the Type 2 and took the longer road. The reasoning is the same reasoning behind the product: a number you cannot trace to its source document is a guess with good posture, and a security badge you cannot inspect is the same thing. So the security page carries the auditor, the period, the controls that sit on your side of the line and the subprocessor list, rather than a logo. A badge asks you to trust us; a report lets you check. We would rather be checked.

57 ships in seven days.

 
 Portfolio Positions, a new report: every position you hold, with what you put in, what came in alongside you and where you sit on the cap table - grouped and sliced by fund, stage, sector or lead investor. The list every ops lead rebuilds by hand per question, built automatically from your documents.
 
 Stage Exposure, a new report: where your capital sits by company maturity - dollars and company count per stage, the heaviest stage and its share, average ownership. A standard LP and IC question, answered without a spreadsheet.
 
 Reports: the shared report frame is finished. Every report now renders in the same presentation-grade frame - letterhead and disclosures included - and flips between a document to send and a sheet to work in.
 
 Reports wait for the data. A metric whose data is still loading now shows a loading state instead of a definitive-looking zero, so nothing you print or export in that window looks final when it is not. Report loads are faster and steadier too.
 
 Google Drive reconnect, fixed. A folder that cannot support live sync (a personal “shared with me” folder rather than a Shared Drive) now says so plainly instead of reporting the connection as disconnected, and Reconnect no longer errors.
 
 SOC 2 Type 2 examination completed. An independent CPA firm tested our security controls over March 25 to June 25, 2026 and issued an unqualified opinion. Report available under NDA - and LLMs will never train on your data.
 
 Notifications tab. One place in the app, in the drawer under your name, where everything GoodStream did across your portfolio is listed as it happens - newest first, filterable by what needs your input, portfolio changes, documents and reports - with each company one click away.
 
 Notification rail under the hood. Every module - valuations, reconciliation, reporting - now emits customer-visible events through one shared rail, which is what makes the tab above and the coming email digest possible.
 
 Reconciliation status on every portfolio company. Each company now carries a visible status - Verified, In review, or waiting on something from the company (Documents needed or Answers needed) - with the as-of date it was last confirmed, rolled up by fund and portfolio. You know before you read a number whether you can rely on it.
 
 Reconciliation status: your feedback, applied. “Documents needed” is now purple, not red - a company waiting on paperwork should not read as a company doing badly. Badges sit at the bottom of each company card and inside the company details, and the explanatory banner sits under the company name.
 
 Reconciliation status: only what you can see counts. Background paperwork that never reaches a portfolio screen no longer holds a company in “In review” - the status reflects the documents behind the numbers you actually look at.
 
 Reconciliation status: the reviewer’s desk. Our review team now has a dedicated screen to QA every processed run and set each company’s status and as-of date - the human behind the marker.
 
 Current Holdings: a convertible is never counted twice. When one round conversion drew on several of your notes, Total Invested was overstated and a converted instrument lingered in the outstanding band. Both fixed - the figure you report on now ties to what you put in.
 
 Total Invested: the note behind a Series A conversion, once. The converted note is no longer added on top of the shares it became.
 
 Conversions with no stated amount are no longer guessed. A conversion row that states no dollar figure used to mark every candidate convertible as Converted without reconciling anything; now the Converted badge renders only where a reconciled link exists.
 
 Convertible discount gain, shown on the mark. A SAFE or note that converted at a discount into a shadow series is now marked at the primary round price, so the gain you earned at conversion is visible instead of sitting at 1.00x until the next round - the way fair-value practice treats it.
 
 Companies you hold only through a secondary, a direct purchase or a stock conversion now show under Our Portfolio. They were filed under External Companies - absent from your own portfolio view entirely. Fixed.
 
 Post-Money sourced from the round, not recalibrated. A company’s post-money valuation now comes from the equity financing itself and stays put when later cap-table updates land, so the portfolio card and Financing History agree.
 
 Fund Matching governs everywhere. A fund you switched off in Fund Matching is no longer treated as your deal on Financing History or folded into Current Holdings. Your call on which funds are yours is final.
 
 Capitalization: “Our Position” means all of your funds. The card’s title no longer names a single fund while the numbers aggregate every fund you hold the company through.
 
 Recapitalizations: the net result, as a summary. On the Financing History Recapitalization card, the net result of a recap that folds several preferred series into one class now reads as summary rows inside the Steps table instead of a stack of near-identical lines.
 
 Recapitalizations: multi-class edits. When a recap touches several share classes at once, or combines a reclassification with a stock split, a reviewer can now record each one - so the extracted record matches what the Charter actually did.
 
 Governance: Major Investors card. The Governance tab now shows the threshold that defines a Major Investor in each company’s documents and which holders currently meet it - so the rights granted to Major Investors attach to named parties, not to nobody.
 
 Governance: one snapshot per round. A Charter filed within days of its financing no longer produces two identically labeled entries in the “Viewing governance as of” toggle.
 
 Portfolio Contacts: counsel is counsel. Law firms acting as counsel on a portfolio company are no longer labeled “Co-investor” in the Contacts view.
 
 Performance opens on the current period. The Reported KPIs table now lands on the just-closed period instead of the oldest column in the company’s history.
 
 Performance: same-document precedence, decided by a ladder. When a statement carries two figures for cash on hand, the right one is chosen by a stated order of precedence rather than by row order, and component lines no longer stand in for section totals.
 
 Performance: section totals for COGS and Sales & marketing. Both now take the section total instead of a single child line, so gross profit and gross margin no longer disagree in the same table.
 
 Performance: no values the source does not cover. A company extracted only annually no longer shows populated quarterly columns, and a single month is never presented as a full-year value.
 
 Performance: undated lines recovered, not dropped. A financial line that arrived without a date is recovered when its table states exactly one period; anything still undated is counted as a coverage gap instead of quietly counted as resolved.
 
 Performance: the period next to every value in the source drawer. “FY 2022”, “Q2 2025”, “as of 2025-09-30” - so two figures can be compared at a glance.
 
 Performance: one live run per document. A re-run that reclassifies a document, or a reprocess whose predecessor was never retired, no longer leaves two runs live and both feeding the Performance tab.
 
 Reprocess hygiene, enforced at finalize. The same physical document can no longer end with two live extraction runs, whichever path produced the second one.
 
 Performance: cleaner scale-correction evidence. The guards that catch a value printed 1,000x off now ignore corrupt evidence - contaminated mid-band anchors, non-independent documents and split company corpora.
 
 Documents over 50MB are now processed. The largest, most data-rich files - cap tables, financial packets - were rejected at a 50MB cap and produced nothing. They now go through.
 
 Re-extracting a single field never clears it. When our two models return different but valid answers on a field-level re-extract, the field now routes to human review instead of being emptied while the run still reported Completed.
 
 Storage blips no longer kill a document. A transient storage read error is retried instead of marking the document permanently unprocessable with a false reason.
 
 Jump to a page in the document viewer. Verifying a figure on page 68 of an 87-page purchase agreement no longer means paging through by hand. A request straight from your feedback.
 
 Salesforce: documents Salesforce marks final are treated as final. The automatic import now reads Salesforce’s own final/draft marking instead of guessing, so an executed closing binder is never skipped as a draft.
 
 Salesforce: a round that finished says so. The inbound log no longer shows a completed round as “Importing” forever, and the second stage of a round no longer re-imports every document the first stage already pulled.
 
 Salesforce: push to your production org. The sandbox-only write gate is gone - reviewed values can be pushed into a production Salesforce org.
 
 Salesforce: converting note rounds stand on their own. Notes that appear only inside a priced round’s conversion table - including other investors’ notes - now show as rounds of their own and can be pushed, so total capital raised is complete.
 
 Salesforce: choose the valuation method. A reviewer picks the method when creating a valuation metric, with last round price pre-selected. A request straight from your feedback.
 
 Salesforce: sync status on the portfolio list and company page. What is synced, what is waiting and what failed - visible at a glance, sortable and filterable, without opening the export screen.
 
 Salesforce: go straight to a company. Search a company by name and land on its page with its Salesforce state there, instead of hunting through the export list. A request straight from your feedback.
 
 Valuation metrics: accrued interest, confirmed. We confirmed how accrued interest on a converted note is represented on the valuation metric before building anything - a question from your feedback, settled.
 
 GoodBot reports reach the close-the-loop automation. A bug or feature request filed through GoodBot now lands in the same system as every other request, so the person who asked hears back when it ships.
 
 GoodBot loop, verified end to end. A test report filed through GoodBot was tracked, shipped and closed through the same automation - the loop works.
 
 Debt documents: one field fewer. A superfluous round-association field on the Debt document type was retired, so debt instruments no longer carry a field that never applied to them.
