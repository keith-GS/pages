Why Not a TOTO Washlet S7A?

The S7A is the gold standard of bidet seats in the US market - $1,499, premist, ewater+, instant heating, the slimmest rear profile, the most refined remote. If your toilets were standard US ceramics, this is exactly what I'd recommend. But.

TOTO uses 5.5" seat-bolt spacing, which is the US standard. Duravit and Geberit bowls do not always match it. Amazon Q&A, the official TOTO Washlet Fit Chart, and several Houzz threads all confirm this is a real fit risk on European wall-hung ceramics. Could it work? Maybe. Could it look bolted-on and have a hose that doesn't tuck cleanly? Also yes. For the same money as a SensoWash Slim, you'd be taking on fit risk on a $1,500 purchase, in a Manhattan apartment where having the installer come back twice costs you a whole afternoon. The SensoWash Slim is the lower-risk move.

For the upstairs Geberit, the S7A is even more wrong because it doesn't integrate with the Sigma carrier - you'd still see a hose and a cord, and you'd have paid for an electrician anyway. Spend the money on a Mera instead.

Before You Order - 10-Minute Verification

Three things to confirm in person before any of these models ships. Doing this now saves a return + restock + reinstall cycle.

  1. Downstairs Duravit model number. Look under the bowl rim or on the back near the wall for a 6-digit Duravit part number. The SensoWash Slim fits Starck 2/3, ME by Starck, Darling New, DuraStyle, and Vero Air. It does not fit Happy D., D-Code, or Vero (older) without switching to the Starck f or DuraStyle SensoWash variant. If you can pull the number, send it - happy to confirm fit before you click buy.
  2. Upstairs Geberit Sigma generation. Pop the flush plate off the wall (it lifts off, no tools, just gentle upward pressure). Behind it you'll see the cistern marked Sigma 8, Sigma 12, or Sigma 70. AquaClean Mera retrofits to Sigma 8 and 12. Anything older (pre-2010 Sigma 30) is a different upgrade path.
  3. Upstairs water supply tap. While the flush plate is off, look for a small capped outlet on the left side of the cistern. That is the bidet feed Geberit pre-plumbs. If it's there, the retrofit is straightforward. If not, the plumber installs an angle stop - still doable, just adds 30 minutes.

Install Considerations

Water supply

Plumber required

Both toilets have their water supply inside the wall via concealed cistern. A plumber removes the flush plate, installs a bidet stopcock on the cistern's secondary outlet, and runs flex tubing to the seat. ~1-3 hours total for both rooms.

Electrical

Upstairs only - electrician

Downstairs has a GFCI already. Upstairs needs power pulled from the sconce above the toilet-paper holder. ~2 hours with a residential electrician. Tip them to add a GFCI receptacle behind the toilet so it's invisible.

Find the right installer

Brand-trained plumber

Geberit and Duravit both maintain dealer/installer locators. Manhattan has Geberit-trained plumbers - use one. A generic handyman who's never seen a Sigma cistern will charge double and still call support mid-job.

Order in the right sequence

Confirm model fit FIRST

Verify the three items in the checklist above before placing hardware orders. SensoWash Slim and AquaClean Mera both have restocking fees if returned. Twenty minutes of upfront verification saves a $500 mistake.

The Honest Take

You picked the right house to put bidets in. Wall-hung concealed-cistern toilets are the one architecture where premium bidet hardware actually looks built into the bathroom rather than added to it - which is exactly what you want in a Chelsea apartment that has the leopard wallpaper energy going. The catch is that the install is a one-time event involving real tradespeople, not an Amazon-and-screwdriver weekend. Plan for half a day of plumber on-site, one electrician visit upstairs, and budget the install at roughly 25-30% on top of hardware.

If you had to pick only one room to do first, do the downstairs. The Duravit + SensoWash Slim path is the lower-friction, faster, less-expensive of the two - GFCI already there, no ceramic swap, hardware is one box. You'll get the daily quality-of-life upgrade and you can decide on the upstairs Mera-vs-Tuma question with a week of real-world data.

One last thing. Both of these are 110-120V appliances on hardwired or plugged-in power. Run them on the GFCI circuit (downstairs is already that way; the new upstairs run should be a dedicated GFCI feed). Bathroom code in NYC is enforced and the inspector will not approve a non-GFCI bidet outlet.
